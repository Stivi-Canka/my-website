Open weights are being used as a pricing weapon; analog silicon is the long-run cost floor

Open weights · Jul 27 2026

Frontier scores, published weights, custom licence

Moonshot released Kimi K3's weights on 27 July: 2.8 trillion total parameters with 104 billion active, 16 of 896 experts routed per token, 69 Kimi Delta Attention plus 24 Gated MLA layers, and a 1,048,576-token context. It is not uniformly behind the closed labs — 93.5 on GPQA Diamond against Claude Opus 4.8's 91.0 and 91.2 on BrowseComp against Fable 5's 88.0, though 67.5 on DeepSWE against GPT-5.6 Sol's 73.0. The weights ship under Moonshot's own "Kimi K3 License", not a permissive one.

The commercial consequence, in Nathan Lambert's phrasing, is that strong open weights "massively reduce the margin potential for the closed labs" — which starves reinvestment and marks down terminal value, weakening the next fundraise. Read that against section 04, where a chipmaker and a state fund, not venture firms, are setting the prices.

Open weights · March 2026

The same squeeze reached voice a quarter earlier

Mistral's Voxtral TTS took 68.4% against ElevenLabs Flash v2.5's 31.6% in a zero-shot voice-cloning preference test, claims parity with ElevenLabs v3 on emotion steering, generates a ten-second sample in about 70 ms, and lists at $0.016 per thousand characters — against a business built on per-character pricing.

The detail worth holding: the weights are CC BY-NC 4.0. Nobody can legally self-host them to compete commercially, so the pressure on ElevenLabs comes from Mistral's own hosted price, with the open release doing the marketing. That is a pricing strategy wearing an open-source licence, and it is the more likely template.

Analog compute · Oct 2025 – Mar 2026

Memristors do the multiply-accumulate in physics

A memristor holds a weight as physical conductance, so a crossbar of them computes rather than stores: drive a voltage along each row, Ohm's law performs the multiply, Kirchhoff's law sums the currents down the column, and the result appears without the weights ever moving to a compute unit. Data movement, not arithmetic, is where most of the energy and latency in GPU inference goes — which is why in-memory compute is discussed in orders of magnitude rather than percentages.

Two results moved it closer to real. A DGIST-led group reported wafer-scale fabrication of passive memristive crossbars in Nature Communications at roughly 95% average device yield on 4-inch wafers, using CMOS-compatible steps without high-temperature processing — manufacturing, not physics, has been the blocker. And a bismuth-selenide crossbar (gold–Bi2Se3–titanium junctions) ran a fully analog controller on about 7 µW with under 1% conductance drift over 10,000 seconds. The honest distance: that controller balanced a lever, not a transformer, and a 4-inch wafer is not 300-millimetre production.
